Q23. Read the extract below and answer the questions:

“In Mesopotamian tradition, city life was epitomised by the Epic of Gilgamesh. Gilgamesh was the legendary king of Uruk, a mighty hero who conquered distant lands. When his beloved companion Enkidu died, Gilgamesh fell into deep despair and wandered the earth in search of the secret of immortality. After defying deadly monsters and crossing the waters of death, he failed in his quest to conquer mortality. Exhausted and resigned, Gilgamesh returned to his beloved city of Uruk. There, to console himself, he walked around the magnificent city walls, admiring the foundations made of baked brick. He looked out at the city: one-third city, one-third date-grove, one-third clay-pit, and the temple precinct of Inanna. He took pride in the monumental city that would outlive his mortal body.”

  1. Who was Gilgamesh, and which great city did he rule? (1 mark)
  2. What tragedy prompted Gilgamesh to embark on his quest across the earth? (1 mark)
  3. How does the conclusion of the Epic of Gilgamesh reflect the psychological importance of cities to the ancient Mesopotamians? (2 marks)

Q24. Read the extract below and answer the questions:

“Early Mesopotamians attached great value to written tablets. The Assyrian king Ashurbanipal (668–627 BCE) collected a vast library at his capital, Nineveh. He sent his scribes across southern Mesopotamia to copy ancient texts on omen literature, astrology, rituals, medicine, and epics. Each tablet carried a colophon: ‘I, Ashurbanipal, king of the world, king of Assyria, wrote on tablets, checked, and collated this wisdom for my royal reading.’ Centuries later, Nabonidus, the last king of Babylon, was an antiquarian and archaeologist. When excavating the foundations of the ancient sun-temple at Sippar, he repaired the collapsed statues of ancient kings who had ruled a thousand years before him, recording that he did so to preserve the ancestral memory of the land.”

  1. Which Assyrian capital was home to the legendary royal library of Ashurbanipal? (1 mark)
  2. What is a “colophon” on a cuneiform tablet as described in the text? (1 mark)
  3. What do the actions of Ashurbanipal and Nabonidus demonstrate about the Mesopotamian attitude toward their written heritage and historical memory? (2 marks)
