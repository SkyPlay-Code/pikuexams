Q30. Read the extract below and answer the questions:

“The Emperor Trajan (98–117 CE) was the last great conqueror of Rome. In an ambitious military campaign across the Euphrates between 113 and 117 CE, he occupied Armenia and marched down the Tigris river, capturing the Parthian capital of Ctesiphon. He sailed down to the head of the Persian Gulf. The historian Cassius Dio relates that Trajan stood on the shore of the Persian Gulf, watching a merchant vessel sailing for India. Trajan wept, lamenting that he was too old to repeat the exploits of Alexander the Great. Yet, only a few years after his death, his successor Hadrian abandoned all of Trajan’s conquests east of the Euphrates, recognizing that holding Mesopotamia was an unsustainable military liability.”

  1. Which geographical territories were conquered by Trajan during his eastern campaign? (1 mark)
  2. Why did Emperor Trajan weep on the shores of the Persian Gulf? (1 mark)
  3. Why did Trajan’s successor, Hadrian, immediately abandon these newly conquered eastern territories? Explain the underlying geopolitical logic. (2 marks)

Q31. Read the extract below and answer the questions:

“Doctor Galen, a famous Greek physician practicing in Rome, wrote about the seasonal famines that struck Mediterranean cities in the second century CE: ‘The city-dwellers, according to their custom, collected and stored up in their granaries enough grain to last through the next whole year immediately after the harvest. The country-dwellers were left with pulses, beans, and whatever crops were considered second-rate. After consuming these in the winter, the peasants were forced to feed on unwholesome vegetation during the spring; they ate twigs, shoots of trees, bulbs, and unpalatable roots. Many peasants developed swelling of legs and bodies, lethal ulcers, and died of dropsy, while the city granaries remained locked and full of wheat.’”

  1. Who was Galen, and which phenomenon does he describe in this passage? (1 mark)
  2. How did urban elites protect themselves from food shortages at the direct expense of rural peasants? (1 mark)
  3. What does this text reveal about the deep structural inequality and exploitation between Roman cities and rural countryside? (2 marks)

Q32. Read the extract below and answer the questions:

“Saint Augustine of Hippo (354–430 CE), one of the greatest Christian theologians, spent most of his life in Roman North Africa. In his autobiographical writings, he gives a vivid picture of domestic life and patriarchal authority. He describes how his devout mother, Monica, was regularly beaten by his hot-tempered pagan father, Patricius. Augustine noted that in the town where they lived, most wives displayed bruises and lacerations on their faces from domestic violence. Yet, the legal framework granted women significant property independence. A Roman wife did not come under the marital power of her husband; her dowry was managed separately, and upon her father’s death, she became an independent property owner who could buy, sell, or bequeath estates as she pleased.”

  1. Who was Saint Augustine, and which province of the Roman Empire was he from? (1 mark)
  2. What stark contradiction between legal property rights and domestic social reality is highlighted in this passage? (1 mark)
  3. How did the institution of the Roman dowry and inheritance laws protect a woman’s economic standing within a marriage? (2 marks)
