Q30. Read the extract below and answer the questions:

“The Rule of Saint Benedict, compiled in 529 CE, contains 73 chapters outlining the daily routine of monastic life. Chapter 48 dictates: ‘Idleness is the enemy of the soul. Therefore, the brothers should have specified periods for manual labor as well as for prayerful reading.’ Monks woke up before dawn for Matins and prayed seven times a day. Between services, they cleared forests, drained marshes, cultivated fields, copied classical Latin manuscripts in the scriptorium, and tended to sick travelers in the hospice. Monasteries admitted both men and women (who entered nunneries as nuns). Monks took vows of poverty, chastity, and obedience to the Abbot. However, by the 14th century, critics like the English poet Geoffrey Chaucer satirized monasteries, noting that many abbots lived like wealthy lords, wore fur-trimmed cloaks, kept hunting dogs, and neglected manual labor.”

  1. Who established the monastic code described here, and in which year? (1 mark)
  2. Mention two daily manual or intellectual activities performed by Benedictine monks. (1 mark)
  3. How did the original ascetic ideals of early monasticism change by the 14th century, as reflected in contemporary literature? (2 marks)

Q31. Read the extract below and answer the questions:

“From the twelfth century, large churches—called Cathedrals—were being built in France. These belonged to monasteries or urban bishoprics. Cathedrals were built of stone and took many decades or even centuries to complete. Different groups contributed to their construction with their labor, materials, or money. Cathedrals were designed so that the priest’s voice could be heard clearly throughout the huge hall, and singing by the choir sounded glorious. Stained glass windows were an exquisite feature. During the day, sunlight streaming through the stained glass made them radiant; after dark, candlelight lit them up from within. For an illiterate population who could not read the Latin Bible, these stained-glass windows served as visual scriptures, narrating scenes of the creation, the life of Jesus, and the lives of saints.”

  1. What were Cathedrals, and who financed or contributed to their construction? (1 mark)
  2. Identify two distinct architectural and visual features of Gothic cathedrals mentioned in the text. (1 mark)
  3. How did stained-glass windows function as an educational and religious medium for common medieval Europeans? (2 marks)

Q32. Read the extract below and answer the questions:

“The ‘New Monarchs’—Louis XI in France, Maximilian in Austria, Henry VII in England, and Ferdinand and Isabella in Spain—were monarchs who started an organized process of centralizing royal power in the late 15th and early 16th centuries. They dismissed feudal levies and organized permanent, professional standing armies equipped with modern gunpowder, siege artillery, and muskets. The feudal nobility, who previously defied the king with private knight armies and fortified stone castles, found their stone battlements easily shattered by royal cannons. Monarchs introduced centralized taxation, bypassed feudal courts with royal judges, and recruited educated urban middle-class lawyers and merchants into their royal councils instead of traditional aristocrats.”

  1. Name any two rulers categorized as the “New Monarchs” in late medieval Europe. (1 mark)
  2. What military innovation rendered the traditional feudal knights and stone castles obsolete? (1 mark)
  3. How did the New Monarchs systematically dismantle the independent political dominance of the feudal nobility? (2 marks)
