Q30. Read the extract below and answer the questions:

“Giovanni Pico della Mirandola (1463–1494), a brilliant humanist philosopher of Florence, wrote the celebrated essay ‘Oration on the Dignity of Man’. In this text, he imagines God speaking to Adam at the dawn of creation: ‘We have given you, O Adam, no fixed seat, no form of your own, nor any special gift, so that you may choose whatever seat, whatever form, and whatever gifts you desire. All other creatures have their nature defined and restricted within laws established by Us. You alone, constrained by no limits, may determine your nature for yourself according to your own free will. You may degenerate into lower brutish forms of life; or you may, through your soul’s judgment, be reborn into the higher divine orders.’”

  1. Who wrote the Oration on the Dignity of Man, and which city did he belong to? (1 mark)
  2. According to the author, what fundamental gift distinguishes human beings from all other creatures? (1 mark)
  3. How does this passage reflect the central Renaissance ideals of individualism, human agency, and free will, in contrast to medieval theological determinism? (2 marks)

Q31. Read the extract below and answer the questions:

“Martin Luther was appalled by the sale of Indulgences (letters of papal pardon) sold by the Dominican friar Johann Tetzel in Germany. Tetzel told credulous peasants: ‘As soon as the coin in the coffer rings, the soul from purgatory springs.’ On 31 October 1517, Luther posted his 95 Theses on the door of the All Saints’ Church in Wittenberg, challenging the Pope’s authority to sell forgiveness for sins. Luther insisted that scripture (the Bible) was the sole authority on Christian doctrine, not the Pope or church councils. To make the scriptures accessible to common people who knew no Latin, Luther translated the New Testament into vernacular German in 1522. Within a few months, over 200,000 copies of his German translation were printed and distributed across the German principalities.”

  1. What was an “Indulgence”, and why did Johann Tetzel sell them to ordinary people? (1 mark)
  2. Where and when did Martin Luther publicize his 95 Theses? (1 mark)
  3. How did Luther’s translation of the Bible into the vernacular, combined with the printing press, democratize religious knowledge and ignite the Protestant Reformation? (2 marks)

Q32. Read the extract below and answer the questions:

“In 1633, the Italian astronomer Galileo Galilei was summoned to Rome by the Inquisition. In his work ‘Dialogue Concerning the Two Chief World Systems’, Galileo marshaled direct telescopic evidence: the moons orbiting Jupiter, sunspots on the solar surface, and the phases of Venus—all proving that the Earth moves around the Sun, confirming Copernicus’s heliocentric theory. The Catholic Church condemned the Copernican view as formal heresy, because several biblical passages (such as the Book of Joshua) described the Sun as being commanded to stand still, implying that the Sun moves around a stationary Earth. Broken by physical illness and the threat of torture, the 69-year-old Galileo was forced to kneel, publicly abjure his scientific discoveries, and spend the remainder of his life under strict house arrest.”

  1. Which scientific instrument did Galileo invent or refine to provide empirical evidence for the heliocentric theory? (1 mark)
  2. Why did the Roman Catholic Church consider the Copernican heliocentric theory to be dangerous heresy? (1 mark)
  3. What does the trial of Galileo reveal about the historical confrontation between institutional religious dogma and empirical scientific methodology? (2 marks)
