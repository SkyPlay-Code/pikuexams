Q31. [Theoretical Source Extract — Chapter 4: John Rawls and Distributive Justice]

Read the following analytical text from Chapter 4 of the NCERT textbook and answer the questions that follow:

*"Rawls argues that the only way we can arrive at a fair and just rule is if we imagine ourselves to be in a situation in which we have to make decisions about how society should be organized, but we do not know what position we would ourselves occupy in that society.

We do not know what family we will be born into, whether we will be born rich or poor, upper-caste or lower-caste, healthy or physically challenged, male or female.

Rawls describes this condition as choosing under a ‘veil of ignorance’. He expects that in such a condition of complete ignorance about our future identities, each person would decide the rules from the perspective of the worst-off member of society.

Because anyone could end up being the poorest or most disadvantaged person, self-interest dictating risk-aversion leads people to ensure that the least privileged group gets maximum protection and resources."*

Sub-Questions:

  1. Name the conceptual thought experiment described in the passage that strips individuals of their social identities. [1 Mark]
  2. Why does rational self-interest compel individuals behind this veil to worry about the position of the worst-off member of society? [1 Mark]
  3. Evaluate how John Rawls cleverly derives an ethical defense of social welfare and redistribution from rational self-interest rather than relying on abstract altruism or religious charity. [2 Marks]

Q33. [Applied Ethical Dilemma — Distributive Justice in Healthcare]

Policy Dilemma Scenario:

This funding will provide free clean drinking water purification units, generic child vaccinations, basic anti-malarial medicines, and maternity care to millions of impoverished families living below the poverty line, but will not fund any high-tech tertiary care.

                             [ BUDGETARY DILEMMA ]
  
     Proposal Alpha: ₹1,000 Cr -> 2 High-Tech Super-Specialty Research Hospitals.
     Proposal Beta:  ₹1,000 Cr -> 5,000 Primary Health Clinics for the poor.
