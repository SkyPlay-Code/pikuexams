Q30. [Philosophical Source Extract — Chapter 5: Human Dignity and Rights]

Read the following analytical text from Chapter 5 of the NCERT textbook and answer the questions that follow:

*"To understand why we have rights, we must understand what it means to be human. Immanuel Kant, the German moral philosopher, argued that human beings, unlike objects, possess intrinsic value. A table or a pen has a market price; it can be replaced by an equivalent object. Human beings, however, possess dignity.

Because humans possess dignity, they must be treated as ends in themselves and never as instruments or means to achieve someone else’s selfish goals.

It is from this foundational moral premise that the modern doctrine of Human Rights emerges.

To respect a person’s dignity means guaranteeing them the freedom to make autonomous moral choices, providing them with the material resources to survive, and protecting them from physical humiliation and bondage.

Rights are not concessions granted by a benevolent monarch; they are the necessary social conditions that recognize the equal moral worth of every human life."*

Sub-Questions:

  1. According to Immanuel Kant, what is the crucial philosophical difference between a material object and a human being? [1 Mark]
  2. What does Kant’s ethical command to treat people as “ends in themselves” imply for how a state must treat its citizens? [1 Mark]
  3. Analyze how grounding rights in universal human dignity provides a more robust defense against state tyranny than traditional theories of “customary” or “legal” rights. [2 Marks]

Q31. [Legal Source Extract — Chapter 6: The Struggle of Urban Slum Dwellers]

Read the following adapted judicial excerpt from the landmark judgment of the Supreme Court of India in Olga Tellis v. Bombay Municipal Corporation (1985) and answer the questions that follow:

*"The sweep of the right to life conferred by Article 21 is wide and far-reaching. It does not mean merely that life cannot be extinguished or taken away as, for example, by the imposition and execution of the death sentence, except according to procedure established by law. That is but one facet of the right to life.

An equally important facet of that right is the right to livelihood because no person can live without the means of living, that is, the means of livelihood.

If the right to livelihood is not treated as a part of the constitutional right to life, the easiest way of depriving a person of his life would be to deprive him of his means of livelihood to the point of abrogation.

The eviction of a person from a pavement or slum will inevitably lead to the deprivation of his means of livelihood and consequently to the deprivation of life.

The state cannot evict impoverished citizens from public lands without providing them with reasonable notice, an opportunity to be heard, and alternative rehabilitation."*

Sub-Questions:

  1. Identify the constitutional article interpreted by the Supreme Court in the Olga Tellis case. [1 Mark]
  2. What essential economic right did the Supreme Court explicitly link to the Right to Life in this judgment? [1 Mark]
  3. Evaluate how this landmark ruling deepened the substantive meaning of Indian Citizenship for marginalized urban populations who live in illegal squatter settlements and pavements. [2 Marks]
