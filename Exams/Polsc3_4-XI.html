Q36. [Case Study Extract — Chapter 3: Election and Representation]

Read the following extract carefully and answer the questions that follow:

*"No system of election can be completely ideal. In a diverse country like India, the choice of the electoral system has far-reaching consequences for social harmony and the representation of various groups. During the deliberations of the Constituent Assembly, there were intense debates regarding the adoption of proportional representation versus the first past the post system.

Members like K.T. Shah and Z.H. Lari advocated for proportional representation, arguing that it guarantees representation to all political currents and minorities according to their numerical strength.

However, Dr. B.R. Ambedkar and Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel firmly defended the majority system (FPTP). They contended that proportional representation introduces complexity that would baffle an illiterate electorate, produces fragile multi-party coalitions incapable of taking decisive nation-building measures, and destroys the territorial link between a constituent and their representative. The ultimate constitutional compromise adopted FPTP for general legislative assemblies while preserving proportional representation for indirect elections to second chambers."*

Sub-Questions:

  1. State the primary argument advanced by K.T. Shah and Z.H. Lari in favor of Proportional Representation during the Constituent Assembly debates. [1 Mark]
  2. Why did Dr. B.R. Ambedkar and Sardar Patel consider the FPTP system indispensable for India’s legislative lower houses? [1 Mark]
  3. Evaluate how the Indian constitutional architecture achieved a middle path between proportional fairness and executive stability through its institutional arrangements. [2 Marks]
