“In fact, I share the sentiments that such articles will never be called into operation and they would remain a dead letter. Q32. [Comparative Source Extract — Nigeria’s Federal Dilemma]

Read the following analytical text from the chapter’s comparative study on Nigerian Federalism and answer the questions that follow:

*"If the regions and the various communities do not trust each other, even a federal arrangement cannot guarantee unity. The experience of Nigeria provides an instructive example. Until 1914, Northern and Southern Nigeria were two separate British territories. In the 1950s, a federal system was created along the lines of the three major regions: the Northern region dominated by the Hausa-Fulani, the Western region dominated by the Yoruba, and the Eastern region dominated by the Igbo.

However, conflict soon erupted over control of central political offices and regional boundaries. In 1966, a military coup took place, and the Eastern region attempted to secede as an independent state named Biafra, precipitating a bloody three-year civil war. Even after redrawing the country into 36 smaller states to dilute ethnic hegemony, tensions persist over the equitable redistribution of petroleum revenues derived from the southern Niger Delta."*

Sub-Questions:

  1. Name the breakaway state that attempted to secede from the Nigerian Federation in 1966, triggering a prolonged civil war. [1 Mark]
  2. What institutional restructuring did Nigerian rulers undertake after the civil war to dilute the political hegemony of the three major ethnic groups? [1 Mark]
  3. Analyze the central lesson that the Nigerian and Soviet experiences provide regarding the limitations of mere legal/constitutional federal structures in societies that lack political democracy and mutual trust among communities. [2 Marks]
