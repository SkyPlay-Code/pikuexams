Q32. [Constituent Assembly Debates Extract — Village Autonomy]

Read the following historical excerpt from the Constituent Assembly Debates and answer the questions that follow:

*"The village had been an integral part of the Indian polity since time immemorial. Yet, when the Draft Constitution was prepared, village panchayats were conspicuous by their absence from the fundamental structure of the proposed republic. Members like K. Santhanam, T. Prakasam, and Mahavir Tyagi expressed bitter anguish.

In contrast, Dr. B.R. Ambedkar mounted a fierce intellectual defense of this omission, stating: ‘What is the village but a sink of localism, a den of ignorance, narrow-mindedness, and communalism? I am glad that the Draft Constitution has discarded the village and adopted the individual as its unit.’

To reconcile these stark, uncompromising ideological extremes, the Constituent Assembly engineered an informal consensus by inserting Article 40 in the non-justiciable Directive Principles, advising the state to organize village panchayats with such powers as may be necessary to enable them to function as units of self-government."*

  2. Name any two members of the Constituent Assembly who vehemently opposed the omission of village panchayats from the original Draft Constitution. [1 Mark]
  3. Evaluate why the constitutional compromise of relegating village panchayats to Article 40 of the Directive Principles of State Policy in 1950 proved largely ineffective for the subsequent four decades, necessitating the 73rd Amendment in 1992. [2 Marks]
