Q30. [Constituent Assembly Debates Extract — Constitution as a Living Charter]

Read the following historical excerpt from the speech of Pandit Jawaharlal Nehru in the Constituent Assembly and answer the questions that follow:

*"While we want this Constitution to be as solid and permanent as we can make it, there is no permanence in a Constitution. There should be a certain flexibility. If you make anything rigid and permanent, you stop the nation’s growth, the growth of a living, vital, organic people…

In any event, we could not make this Constitution so rigid that it cannot be adapted to changing conditions. When the world is in turmoil and we are passing through a very swift period of transition, what we may do today may not be wholly applicable tomorrow.

Therefore, while the basic principles must remain steadfast, the machinery of adaptation must remain open, lest the people break the Constitution to ensure their progress."*
— Jawaharlal Nehru, Constituent Assembly Debates, Vol. VII, November 1948.

Sub-Questions:

  1. State the central warning issued by Jawaharlal Nehru against making a constitution excessively rigid and unalterable. [1 Mark]
  2. What balance did the framers intend to achieve between “steadfast basic principles” and the “machinery of adaptation”? [1 Mark]
  3. Analyze how the amending mechanism under Article 368 has justified Nehru’s vision by allowing the Constitution to evolve as a living document while resisting transient political whims. [2 Marks]

*"Secularism in India did not emerge merely as a legal separation between Church and State. Under conditions of deep religious diversity and entrenched internal hierarchies within religions, a passive state would simply reinforce traditional oppression.

Indian secularism, therefore, rejected both the theocratic model and the classical French or American model of strict non-intervention. It allowed for what political theorists describe as ‘principled distance’.

A secular state may maintain a distance from religion, but it can also intervene to reform regressive religious customs that violate human dignity, such as the prohibition of temple entry for Dalits, child marriage, or discriminatory inheritance laws.

At the same time, it protects the right of religious communities to establish their own educational institutions and preserve their distinct cultural identities."*

  3. Evaluate why the classical Western model of strict mutual exclusion would have proved inadequate to address the social realities of caste oppression and religious discrimination in India. [2 Marks]
