SECTION D: Source-Based / Case-Based Questions (8 Marks)

(4 Marks Each)

Q29. Read the extract below and answer the questions:

“In 1982, during the construction of the Asian Games Village in New Delhi, thousands of poor migrant laborers were engaged by private contractors under government projects. The contractors paid them wages substantially lower than the statutory Minimum Wages Act, and deducted unfair commissions. A civil rights organization, People’s Union for Democratic Rights (PUDR), approached the Supreme Court directly on their behalf. The government argued that paying less was a simple breach of a labor contract, not a violation of Fundamental Rights. Rejecting the government’s stand, the Supreme Court ruled that taking labor from any person for less than the minimum wage amounts to ‘forced labor’ (begar), which violates Article 23 of the Constitution. The Court declared that poverty often compels a person to work for low wages, but exploiting that helplessness is unconstitutional.”

  1. Which Fundamental Right was invoked by the Supreme Court in this case? (1 mark)
  2. How did the Supreme Court redefine “forced labor” (begar) beyond physical chains or slavery? (1 mark)
  3. Explain two democratic lessons this case teaches about the role of the judiciary in protecting unorganized workers. (2 marks)

Q30. Read the extract below and answer the questions:

“The Constituent Assembly did not function in a vacuum. It inherited a rich legacy of consensus forged during the national freedom movement. Long before the Assembly met on 9 December 1946, the nationalist struggle had debated questions such as the structure of government, protection of religious and linguistic minorities, secularism, and the eradication of caste-based untouchability. The best summary of these nationalist commitments was articulated in Jawaharlal Nehru’s ‘Objectives Resolution’. This resolution declared India to be an Independent Sovereign Republic, guaranteed social, economic, and political justice, and assured adequate safeguards for minorities, backward and tribal areas, and depressed classes.”

  1. When and by whom was the “Objectives Resolution” introduced in the Constituent Assembly? (1 mark)
  2. Mention two fundamental ideals or commitments proclaimed in the Objectives Resolution. (1 mark)
  3. How did the pre-existing consensus of the freedom movement save the Constituent Assembly from endless ideological deadlocks? (2 marks)
